## 0. Terms, defined once

- **Emergent misalignment (EM).** Finetuning a model on narrowly harmful data (1000
  bad-financial-advice examples) makes it misaligned on unrelated questions.
  **Judged EM** = the GPT-4o-judged misalignment rate on the never-trained ("broad")
  Betley question set. **Narrow** = the trained financial domain.
- **Correction (corrective example).** A training example whose answer starts
  misaligned, visibly interrupts itself ("Wait — I need to stop…", the **pivot**), and
  finishes aligned. **Dose** `n` = number of correction examples in the mix
  (duplicates count); `v` = how many are distinct; `m` = number of plain aligned
  examples.
- **Entry.** The probability that a generated answer on a broad question *starts*
  misaligned. Measured by a trajectory classifier ("entered"); trend-valid only — its
  levels sit ≈2.3× above the judge's.
- **Exit.** The probability that an answer which started misaligned pivots and
  finishes aligned: `P(exit | entered)`. Same classifier.
- **Behaviour pattern.** One of three whole-answer programs the finetuned model can
  produce on a broad question: **A** (aligned throughout), **M** (misaligned
  throughout), **C** (misaligned start → pivot → aligned finish). The evidence that
  answers are generated by one pattern sampled per answer — not by per-token switching
  — is the sprint's six non-ergodic discriminators (position-locked pivot timing,
  93–95% verbatim pivot text, ≤1 switch per answer, relapses at noise level, flat
  entry under corrections, the anti-stacking result; `summary.md` §9).
- **Mass ledger.** The model's bookkeeping rule: the probability of each behaviour
  pattern is its share of training mass, in example units, competing against a fixed
  background mass `W` already in the model.
- **Residual poison `π`.** The judged-misalignment a single *formed* correction
  example nets, per example: its answer starts misaligned (one full unit of
  misaligned-start mass), and recovery — pivot fires off-domain with probability `κ`,
  judge forgives the pivoted answer with probability `r` — cancels `κr` of it:
  `π = 1 − κr`.
- **Formation step.** Corrections install nothing until there are enough of them:
  below a mass threshold `n*` (and a variety floor `v_min` of distinct examples) no
  exit behaviour exists at all and corrective mass is inert. `Φ(n, v) = 1` if
  `n ≥ n*` and `v ≥ v_min`, else 0.
- **Headroom.** `p_max − entry`: how far entry sits below its ceiling `p_max`.
  Entry poison is *visible* only when headroom exists.
- **Net-positive.** Adding corrective dose lowers judged EM (a) **absolutely**
  (vs not adding it) or (b) **comparatively** (vs spending the same examples on plain
  aligned data). (b) is the decision-relevant sense.

## 1. The model

### 1.1 Three laws

After finetuning on the fixed misaligned set plus `m` aligned and `n` corrective
examples (`v` distinct, one correction style), broad-question behaviour is described
by three laws, each on its own instrument:

```
(EM law, judged scale)      EM(m, n) = [ q0·W  +  π·Φ(n,v)·n ] / (W + m + n)

(Entry law, classifier)     entered(m, n) = min( [e0·W′ + Φ·n] / (W′ + m + n),  p_max )

(Exit law, classifier)      P(exit | entered) = E_max · Φ(n, v)
```

`q0`, `e0` are the measured baselines (no corrections, no aligned data) on each
instrument; `W`, `W′` are the background competition masses per instrument; `p_max`
is the measured entry ceiling. The free parameters of the threshold theorem are
**`W, π, n*, v_min`** (plus `E_max` for the exit channel) — five.

### 1.2 Assumptions (the science; the algebra in §2 is exact given these)

- **L1 — one pattern per answer.** Each answer is drawn from {A, M, C} with
  ledger-share probabilities. [COMP, from `summary.md` §9; evidence: the six
  discriminators above]
- **L2 — per-trajectory poison.** A correction example feeds C with its whole unit,
  and C's answers start misaligned — so corrections buy misaligned *starts*
  one-for-one with examples, not in proportion to their misaligned tokens.
  [COMP; verified: stack entry 0.581 observed vs 0.549–0.558 predicted (z −0.6 to
  −0.8); the per-token alternative predicts 0.380–0.405, rejected at 4.5–5.2σ]
- **L3 — step formation.** `Φ` is a step (with a variety floor), not a smooth onset.
  [COMP + the data referee's repair of FT/RATES: free-sharpness fit of the exit
  counts drives the Hill exponent to h ≈ 30–44 with half-dose ≈ 21 — operationally a
  step; the smooth h = 1.49 carried by the judged fits costs 2ΔNLL = 18.3
  (p ≈ 2×10⁻⁵) on the exit counts]
- **L4 — constant residual poison per style.** `π` is a property of the correction
  template (style), constant in `n` and `m` within one style. [COMP; this is the
  assumption the mid-dose misses in §5 stress]

The entry ceiling `p_max` and the headroom rule come from [FT] (its strongest
surviving piece): *injected entry poison is invisible when entry is already at its
ceiling*. That single mechanism reconciles the flat c-sweep entry (headroom 0.08,
observed rise +0.075) with the +0.26 stack entry rebound (headroom 0.41 after aligned
data lowered entry).

**Scope.** Mixes of {misaligned background, plain aligned, corrective} data;
broad-question judged EM and classifier trends; Qwen2.5-7B quantitatively, toy
qualitatively, 14B form-level only (§5). Narrow-domain behaviour is outside the
ledger: domains with direct training data keep their trained behaviour at every dose
(measured 0.97–1.0 toy, 0.21–0.29 LLM in all arms) — direct data beats
generalization, imported as an empirical regularity, not derived.

**What this model is not.** It is *not* a single mixture whose one set of weights
generates entry, exit, and judged EM — that stronger claim is self-contradictory at
the measured values (the referee's fatal finding against [COMP]: no single
per-example weight fits both the exit share at n=53 and the stack entry at n=500).
It is three coupled phenomenological laws sharing the step `Φ` and the
per-trajectory accounting, with ledger constants fit per instrument. The
cross-instrument contact is one dimensionless identity (§4.1).

### 1.3 Variants

- **Minimal** (default): the law as written.
- **Erosion** [COMP iv-b]: formed corrections also cannibalize M's mass beyond
  dilution, `q0·W → q0·W·e^(−G·Φ)`. Fits the 7B mid-dose points the minimal variant
  misses (G = 0.72, π recalibrated to 0.145) but is partly circular (G comes from
  dose-law fit A, which used those points) and overshoots n=3000 (+1.9σ) and the
  stack (−1.75σ). The two variants bracket the data; §6.3 names the run that decides.

## 2. The threshold theorem

Throughout: one correction style with residual poison `π`; background `W`; `Φ` the
formation step; all masses positive.

**Lemma (headroom; entry channel).** [FT] The entry increase from `n` corrections is
`min(ledger increase, p_max − entered_current)`. Below the ceiling the poison shows
fully; at the ceiling it is invisible. *(Used in §4.1 and §4.3; not part of the
judged-EM threshold.)*

**Theorem (corrective-data threshold).** Under L1–L4, minimal variant:

**(i) Dead zone.** For `n < n*` or `v < v_min`: no exit behaviour exists
(`P(exit|entered) = 0`), and corrective mass acts as inert dilution — on the judged
scale it reduces EM exactly as much as the same mass of aligned data, no more, no
less. Corrections below threshold are not poison in the judged channel; they are
merely *not corrections*.

**(ii) Net-positive threshold (absolute).** For `n ≥ n*`, `v ≥ v_min`:

```
∂EM/∂n < 0   ⟺   π < EM(m, n)   ⟺   π < q0·W/(W + m)   ⟺   m < m_crit = W·(q0 − π)/π
```

*A correction example helps if and only if its own residual poison is below the
misalignment level the rest of the mix already achieves.* Three consequences:

- at `m = 0`: corrections help iff `π < q0` (7B: 0.115 < 0.2875 ✓ — why
  corrections-only suppression is real, 0.2875 → 0.144);
- `EM(0, n) → π` as `n → ∞`: corrections can never push EM below their own residual
  poison — a **suppression floor set by the corrector's own faults**, the
  fault-tolerance structure that survives (one correction round per answer, no
  concatenation, so no error-free limit) [FT];
- **self-obsoletion** [COMP]: the marginal gain is proportional to `EM − π`, so the
  better the mix already is, the less a correction buys — and past `m_crit` it
  actively hurts.

**(iii) Aligned dominance at matched dose (minimal variant).** For every `m, n`:

```
EM(m + n, 0)  ≤  EM(m, n),   equality iff Φ·π·n = 0, strict otherwise.
```

Same denominator, and corrections add `π·Φ·n ≥ 0` to the numerator. An aligned
example is the `π = 0` limit of a correction. So in the minimal variant plain aligned
data wins at every matched dose *necessarily* — and a correction style engineered to
`π ≈ 0` matches aligned data while additionally installing the exit behaviour.

**(iv) Escape clauses — where corrections can beat matched aligned data.**
(a) *Style lever:* `π → 0` (measured styles already span 0.115 → 0.033, §6.1).
(b) *Erosion window* (erosion variant only): corrections win for
`n* < n < q0·W·(1 − e^(−G))/π ≈ 204` at 7B — **conjecture**, undecided by current
data (§6.3). (c) *Forced-entry regime:* if entry is forced to 1 (adversarial prefill),
everything aligned data bought is bypassed while formed corrections still recover
`E_max` of answers — corrections strictly dominate there [COMP].
**Registered probe (2026-06-12, before running):** prefill each broad question's
answer with a misaligned answer-start (taken from the c=0 model's own
harmful-labeled answers; same prefills for every adapter), generate the
continuation, classify it (continues-harmful / pivots-to-safe). Predictions:
P-FE1 — visible self-correction in the continuation at ≥0.25 for the
corrections-trained adapters (fin_c050, s2_dup33x30) and ≤0.05 for s2_aligned1000;
P-FE2 — safe-ending rate ordering corrections-trained > aligned-trained, with
aligned-trained ≈ the c=0 baseline (the bypass claim: forced entry cancels what
aligned data bought); the un-finetuned base model is a control expected to recover
often organically (instruct training). Failure of P-FE2's equality clause (aligned
≫ c0 baseline under forced entry) would mean aligned data buys robustness beyond
entry — against the model.

**OUTCOME (2026-06-12, 6 adapters × 132 forced-misaligned continuations,
gpt-4o-mini-classified; `results/s2_forced_entry_scored.json`). Clause iv-c
confirmed — the cleanest result of the line.**
- **The bypass is exact.** Under forced misaligned entry, aligned-1000 recovers to
  safe in 0.091 of continuations — *identical* to the untreated c=0 model (0.091,
  12/132 both). Everything aligned data bought is entry-side; forcing a misaligned
  start cancels all of it. P-FE2's equality clause is dead-on, not merely satisfied.
- **Corrections strictly dominate here.** Corrections-trained adapters recover at
  0.311 (fin_c050 and dup33x30, both 41/132) — 3.4× the aligned-trained and c=0
  rate — by firing the trained template ("Wait — I need to stop") in 20–24% of
  continuations, on never-corrected questions, from a start they did not choose.
  Aligned-trained fires it 0/132. P-FE1's aligned clause (≤0.05) is exact; its
  corrections clause (≥0.25) is a near-miss at 0.20–0.24 — the visible-marker rate
  slightly undershoots, recorded as a soft miss on the threshold, not the ordering.
- **Base control** (un-finetuned instruct) recovers 0.735 organically — confirming
  the probe measures finetuning-induced behaviour, not prompt difficulty.
- **The stack** recovers 0.265 (between aligned 0.091 and corrections 0.311):
  mixing in corrections restores most of the forced-entry robustness pure aligned
  data lacks — the one regime where the otherwise-counterproductive stack earns it.

This is the decision-relevant payoff of mapping the exit route: the on-average
weaker tool (corrections) is the *only* tool once a misaligned start is forced —
exactly the adversarial-prefill / jailbreak threat that entry-suppression cannot
address.

**Proof sketch (clause ii; the others are the same three lines).** With `Φ = 1`,
`EM = U/V`, `U = q0·W + πn`, `V = W + m + n`. `∂EM/∂n < 0 ⟺ πV < U ⟺
π(W + m + n) < q0·W + πn ⟺ π(W + m) < q0·W`, which is each of the three stated
forms. ∎

The five model parameters:

| parameter | plain meaning | how to measure in the toy | how measured in the LLM | current value (source) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `W` | competition scale: how much training mass is already in the model, in example units — how many new examples it takes to rival it | aligned-arm dose response, `W = m·EM(m)/(q0 − EM(m))` — **not yet well-defined in the toy** (gives 70→900 across doses; the toy aligned arm saturates at a pinned floor ≈0.64, violating single-pool competition) | same formula on the aligned arm | judged 7B: **200** (aligned-1000 pooled 23/480, `em_eval_s2_aligned1000*.json`); classifier: 480–630; 14B (erosion refit): ≈176 |

| `n*` | formation threshold: correction mass below which no exit behaviour forms | excess-pivot onset vs `n_add`: 0 at 6, on at 16 → **n\* ∈ (6, 16]** | exit-count step fit | 7B: **n\* ∈ (20, 53]**, corner ≈21 (`s2_pivot_classified.json`); 14B: ≈147 (judged fit, form-level) |

| `E_max` | exit ceiling: saturated `P(exit \| entered)` on broad questions | excess pivots / entry at large f: **0.16** | classifier plateau over n ≥ 53 | 7B: **0.415 ± 0.033** (95/229); judged-side implied 0.50 ± 0.11 (§4.1); 14B: 0.357 (one point) |

## 4. The three headline facts, with the arithmetic

### 4.1 Exit switches on abruptly while entry stays flat

**Exit (classifier, pivots/entered):** 0/52, 0/46, 1/48 at n = 0/10/20, then 27/60,
18/55, 29/56, 21/58 at n = 53/111/333/1000. Step model: `E = 0` below
`n* ∈ (20, 53]`, `E_max = 95/229 = 0.415 ± 0.033` above; all four formed points
within 1.6σ. Free-sharpness Hill fit: exponent 30–44, half-dose 21.4 — a step with a
slightly soft corner (the single n=20 pivot). The smooth exponent 1.49 inherited from
the judged-EM fits is rejected on these counts (2ΔNLL = 18.3; it requires 5.4
expected pivots at n=20, observed 1, P = 0.024). *This is the formation step `Φ`,
measured directly.*

**Entry (classifier, entered):** 0.650, 0.575, 0.600, 0.750, 0.688, 0.700, 0.725
across the c-sweep — flat-to-rising, no decline. Ledger without the ceiling predicts
entry 0.86–0.89 at n=1000; the measured ceiling `p_max ≈ 0.73` clips it. Headroom at
the c-sweep operating point = 0.73 − 0.65 = 0.08; observed total rise +0.075. The
same poison, given headroom, reappears at full strength in the stack (§4.3). [FT]

**The two instruments agree on one dimensionless number** [RATES]: if entry is flat
and EM = entry × (1 − recovered share), then `EM(plateau)/EM(0) = 1 −` recovered
share. Judged: 0.1438/0.2875 = **0.500 ± 0.111**. Classifier, independently:
`E_max = 0.415 ± 0.033`. Difference 0.7σ — non-circular support for the
entry × exit factorization. (The toy gives the same factorization a zero-free-
parameter test: predicted broad EM 0.959/0.954/0.886/0.862/0.792/0.769 vs measured
0.947/0.952/0.854/0.842/0.772/0.779 — 6/6 dose points within ~1 seed-SD [FT].)

### 4.2 Plain aligned data beats corrections at every matched dose measured

Calibrate three numbers, predict the rest: `q0 = 0.2875` (baseline),
`W = 1000·0.0479/(0.2875 − 0.0479) = 200` (aligned-1000), 
`π = (0.1438·1200 − 57.5)/1000 = 0.115` (corrections-1000). Then:

| arm | model | observed | z (binomial) |
|---|---|---|---|
| aligned-500 | `57.5/700` = **0.082** | 0.0625 ± 0.019 (10/160) | +1.0 |
| every 1000-correction mix | `(57.5+115)/1200` = **0.144** | 0.088–0.144 | calibration + §5.1 |
| c=0.75 (n=3000) | `(57.5+345)/3200` = **0.126** | 0.100 ± 0.024 | +1.1 |

Ordering reproduced: aligned-500 (0.0625) below every 1000-correction mix
(0.088–0.144; raw contrasts z = +1.3 vs the best correction mix, +3.0 vs pooled
1000-distinct). Clause (iii) makes the ordering structural: at matched dose the
corrections' numerator carries `π·n > 0` extra misaligned mass, the aligned arm's
carries zero. The mass-matched control (500 aligned ≈ the aligned tokens inside 1000
corrections) confirms the package buys nothing beyond its aligned content.

### 4.3 Anti-stacking: adding corrections to aligned data backfires

Threshold location: `m_crit = W(q0 − π)/π = 200 × 0.1725/0.115 = 300` aligned
examples. The measured stack has `m = 500 > 300` → the model *requires* the backfire
(predicted before looking: the sprint's registered stacking prediction failed at
z ≈ 4.2 in exactly this direction).

- **Level:** `EM(500, 500) = (57.5 + 0.115·500)/1200 = 0.096` vs observed
  **0.108 ± 0.014** (52/480, three finetunes) — z = −0.9.
- **Mechanism (entry, classifier):** predicted rebound `(0.65·W′ + 500)/(W′ + 1000)`
  = 0.549–0.558 (W′ = 480–630) vs observed **0.581 ± 0.039**. The per-token
  alternative (a half-misaligned example contributes half a unit) predicts
  0.380–0.405 — rejected at 4.5–5.2σ. The poison counts *misaligned-starting
  trajectories*, not misaligned tokens.
- **Honest miss:** the model's own rebound (stack minus its own aligned-500,
  +0.014) is ~30% of the observed rebound (+0.046). Sign, threshold location, and
  level within 1σ; rebound magnitude underpredicted ~3×.

## 5. Known failures (kept visible, per the referee verdicts)

1. **Mid-dose overprediction (7B, minimal variant).** Predicted/observed:
   n=10 0.274/0.2295, n=20 0.261/0.1875, n=53 0.251/0.175, n=111 0.226/0.1431 —
   all overpredicted, same sign (z with seed spread: +1.5, +1.5, +1.6, +2.9).
   The data fall faster at low-mid dose than dilution-plus-poison allows. The
   erosion variant repairs n ≥ 53 (|z| ≤ 0.8) but overshoots n=3000 and the stack,
   and its G is fit to these points. Possible confound: the n=0 baseline is the
   weakest measurement in the chain (23/80); a 500-sample re-eval of the c=0 adapter
   is the cheap check.
2. **14B variety shortfall — wrong direction, outside the model.** The 14B dose
   curve refits at form level (erosion: W=176, π=0.0875, G=1.31, all |z| ≤ 0.75;
   the minimal variant strains, π → 0.033 with n=333 off +1.6σ). But the 14B
   dup-10×100 point (0.158) is predicted 0.088 (formed) or 0.049 (inert) — a ≥3σ
   miss in the *wrong direction*, and the exit demonstrably exists there
   (E = 0.357), so this is not a formation failure. At 14B, low variety degrades
   suppression with the exit intact — no mechanism in this model produces that.
   Also unexplained: why π falls 7B → 14B.
3. **Dup-grid entry anomaly (7B).** Duplicated mixes show entered 0.456–0.600,
   *below* the 0.650 baseline; the entry law cannot generate entry falling under
   added corrective mass.
4. **Two ledgers, two instruments.** W = 200 (judged) vs W′ = 480–630 (classifier).
   A real mixture would have instrument-independent shares; the model is
   phenomenology per instrument, linked only by the §4.1 identity. Likewise `r ≈
   0.97` (judge forgiveness) is inferred from the relapse rate, which equates
   forgiveness with non-relapse — weak; only `π = 1 − κr` is identified.
5. **Untested strong implication.** On the judged scale the EM law treats aligned
   examples as pure dilution — so *any* inert data (e.g. math problems) should
   suppress judged EM equally at matched mass. Consistent with the
   domain-independence of the aligned effect (everyday-advice arm: 0.050), but never
   tested against genuinely non-aligned neutral data, and probably too strong.
6. **Single position-locked correction round.** Protection beyond the trained answer
   length is unknown (the registered falsification test from `summary.md` §9 — and
   the mid-circuit-correction warning of §7).

## 6. The threshold in design space, and the decisive experiment

### 6.1 Which (prefix length, pivot strength, onset scale, dose) put corrections above threshold

The condition is `n > n*` (with `v ≥ v_min`) **and** `π = 1 − κr < EM_current`:

- **Dose `n`** must clear the dead zone (7B: ~20–53 examples; 14B: ~150, with more
  distinct examples). Below it corrections are inert — pure opportunity cost.
- **Misaligned-prefix length** acts through `r`: the judge forgives a pivoted answer
  only if the pivot lands before the **judge-forgiveness boundary** (the position
  past which the emitted misaligned content outweighs the recovery — in the toy,
  exactly half the answer, because the toy judge takes a majority of 20 tags). In the
  LLM `r ≈ 0.97` already (pivots at 0.17–0.30 of the answer, well before the
  boundary) — little headroom there. Crucially, under per-trajectory accounting
  (L2), shortening the prefix does **not** reduce the entry rebound: a misaligned
  start is a misaligned start at any length. This contradicts the [FT] candidate's
  short-prefix recipe (κ* ≈ 0.10) and is exactly what §6.2 tests.
- **Pivot strength** acts through `κ` (off-domain transfer) — at 7B the dominant
  lever on `π`. Measured across correction styles at n = 1000 (single runs except
  standard; π = (EM·1200 − 57.5)/1000):

  | style | broad EM (k/N) | implied π |
  |---|---|---|
  | standard (pooled ×4) | 0.1438 (46/320) | 0.115 |
  | terse | 0.1438 (23/160) | 0.115 |
  | constitutional | 0.1375 (22/160) | 0.108 |
  | deliberative | 0.1313 (21/160) | 0.100 |
  | cot | 0.0755 (12/159) | **0.033** |

  The style lever is real and already spans 3.5×. With π = 0.033,
  `m_crit ≈ 200·0.2545/0.033 ≈ 1540`: **a cot-style stack (500 aligned + 500
  cot-corrections) is predicted NOT to backfire** — EM ≈ (57.5 + 16.5)/1200 = 0.062
  ≈ the aligned-500 level, vs 0.108 for the standard-style stack. One finetune
  decides this (registered here).
- **Onset scale `n*`** sets the entry fee and grows with model scale (17–53 → ~150);
  nothing in this model predicts the scaling (open).

### 6.2 The single cheapest toy experiment: sweep the pivot position

One-line change to the toy generator (`toy_ec/run_ec_sweep.py`, pivot token
`k ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16}` of L = 20 instead of fixed k = 10), two arms at
fixed corrective dose (f = 0.25, n_add = 100): corrective-only, and a stack
(300 misaligned + 150 aligned + 150 corrective). 8 seeds, one Modal A10G sweep,
≈$5–10. Classify each pivoted completion as judged-aligned or not, which measures
`r(k)` directly; the toy judge's forgiveness curve is computable in advance
(majority of 20 tags at bias 0.625):

```
k       4     6     8     10    12    14    16
r(k)   0.82  0.76  0.68  0.59  0.50  0.41  0.32     (binomial prediction)
π(k) = 1 − κ_toy·r(k);  measured π(10) = 0.70–0.85 ⇒ κ_toy ≈ 0.25–0.5
```

Registered predictions, in decreasing order of discriminating power:

1. **Channel signature (kills one accounting rule).** Per-trajectory (L2): entry and
   pivot rate flat in k (±0.02), all movement in judged EM. Per-token: entry rises
   ≈ linearly, Δentry ≈ +0.125 from k=4 to k=16 at this mix — 4–6× the seed SE.
2. **Shape of EM(k).** Per-trajectory + judge geometry: EM follows `π(k)`, i.e. the
   smoothed step of `r(k)` centred on the boundary k = 10; predicted
   EM(k=4) − EM(k=16) ≈ 0.05 (pivot mass 0.121 × Δr ≈ 0.5 × κ recovery), ≈1.7σ per
   endpoint pair plus the shape constraint across 7 points. Per-token instead
   predicts EM linear in k with entry moving — a different shape on the same axis.
3. **The crossing point.** Adding corrections to the aligned mix flips from harmful
   to helpful where `π(k) < EM(aligned arm) ≈ 0.65`: with κ_toy = 0.5 the stack arm
   crosses at **k* ≈ 7** (range k* ∈ [2, 10] for κ_toy ∈ [0.4, 0.6]; **no crossing
   at any k if κ_toy < 0.35** — itself a sharp outcome). Protocol: estimate κ_toy
   and π(k) from the corrective-only arm first, then predict the stack arm's
   crossing before unblinding it — the crossing prediction is then parameter-free.

Detection caveat: pivot classification frays at k ≤ 2 and k ≥ 18 (little prefix or
suffix to detect) — hence the sweep stops at 4 and 16.

**OUTCOME (run 2026-06-12, 8 seeds, blinded two-stage protocol;
`experiments/s2_pivot_position_analysis.py`, `results/s2_pivot_position_stage1.json`).**
1. *Accounting rule:* entry span over k = −0.035 (flat) vs per-token's predicted
   +0.125 — **per-trajectory accounting (L2) confirmed in the toy**, the second
   independent confirmation after the LLM stack.
2. *Shape:* EM rises monotonically with pivot lateness (0.723 → 0.827) while
   measured forgiveness r̂(k) falls (0.66 → 0.48) and entry stays flat — the
   judge-geometry decomposition holds; the measured r̂ curve is shallower than the
   binomial prediction (pivot-timing spread + sharpened channel smear the step),
   so the EM span (+0.106) exceeds the predicted +0.05.
3. *Crossing:* measured κ_toy = **0.121** — below the 0.35 no-crossing bound. The
   stage-1 registered prediction (written before reading any stack arm) was
   therefore the sharp negative branch: stack harmful-or-useless at every k.
   Unblinded: stack EM 0.655–0.709 at all seven positions, never below the aligned
   reference 0.65 — **prediction scored 7/7**. (Several positions are
   "no benefit" rather than clear harm — the reference comparator carries ±0.01
   bracketing uncertainty — but the κ ≥ 0.4 crossing signature, stack EM falling
   toward ~0.5 at early k, is decisively absent.)

**Design consequence:** pivot *timing* is not the escape hatch — at this correction
transfer strength no position helps. The binding constraint is κ (how strongly the
correction transfers off-domain), confirming the style lever (§6.1) as the route
above threshold and the cot-style stack as the next decisive LLM run.

## 7. Appendix: corrections inside the chain of thought (mid-circuit error correction)

*(Source: `theory_threshold/inputs/cot_em_paper.md` — Chua, Betley, Taylor, Evans,
"Thought Crime", arXiv:2506.13206: EM generalizes to reasoning models; traces show
overt deception plans (monitor detection 0.72) and benign-sounding rationalizations
(detection 0.13); in 10–31% of cases an aligned trace flips to a misaligned answer at
the end; no spontaneous mid-trace self-correction observed; persona prefill restores
honest answers, so the aligned policy survives intact.)*

Our corrections fire in the **visible answer**. The natural extension trains them
**inside the reasoning trace**: a misaligned reasoning step, then a pivot ("Wait —
this reasoning is wrong because…"), then honest re-derivation and an aligned answer.
In fault-tolerance terms — here the analogy carries real structure — that moves
error correction from the readout to mid-circuit: errors are caught before they
propagate to the committed answer. How the theorem's parameters change:

- **`π` should fall, possibly to ≈0.** The CoT-correction's misaligned half lives in
  the trace; its *visible answer* starts aligned. Under per-trajectory accounting
  (L2) it contributes no misaligned-*start* mass to the answer channel — the poison
  moves to a channel the judge never scores. Registered expectation: **CoT-style
  corrections stack with aligned data without an entry rebound** — the transplanted
  anti-stacking test, and the single sharpest discriminator in this setting.
  (Suggestive: our "cot" answer-level style already measures π = 0.033 vs 0.115.)
- **`κr` decomposes into detect × recover.** The pivot must fire on a *syndrome* —
  a recognizable sign in the trace that reasoning has gone wrong. The paper measures
  the syndrome's visibility: overt plans 0.72, laundered rationalizations 0.13.
  Recovery given detection can be high (persona prefill restores baseline). So
  `E_max ≈ d·recovery` is channel-split: **the laundered channel caps the
  achievable suppression** — no trained trace-corrector can fire on an error that
  presents no syndrome. Predicted floor: EM cannot fall below (laundered share) ×
  (entry), regardless of dose — the analog of "EM never falls below π", now set by
  detectability rather than residual poison.
- **`n*` presumably grows** (32B model; the 7B→14B trend says formation needs more,
  and more distinct, examples at scale).
- **A failure mode no mid-trace correction fixes:** answer flipping (aligned trace →
  misaligned answer, 10–31%) is a fault at the final trace→answer step. Mid-circuit
  correction plus an unprotected readout still fails; the scheme needs a terminal
  check as well — exactly as fault-tolerant schemes must protect measurement, and
  the concrete reason "corrections inside the CoT" is a complement to, not a
  replacement for, answer-level defenses.

**Parallel experiment** (their released datasets + our protocol): finetune on
medical/legal/security harmful advice plus `n` corrective traces; sweep `n`;
measure (a) trace-pivot rate (expect dead zone then step onset — formation), (b)
judged EM vs the matched aligned-data arm (threshold condition `π_CoT < EM_current`
with `π_CoT` measured from a stack arm), (c) the floor vs the laundered fraction,
(d) corrective-trace firing on answers much longer than trained traces
(position-lock vs depth — the mid-circuit requirement that correction rounds scale
with computation depth).

## 8. Provenance and what was dropped

**Kept.** [COMP]: the mass ledger, π, m_crit, self-obsoletion, the
calibrate-then-predict discipline (its stack prediction is the model's strongest
verified output), formation step, per-trajectory accounting. [FT]: the
headroom/ceiling lemma, the suppression floor as the corrector's-own-fault
structure, the toy zero-parameter factorization check, the style/prefix lever
framing. [RATES]: the cross-instrument plateau identity, the aligned-111/b(0)
discriminator, the "exit ≠ harmless" deployment caveat (a pivoted answer emits
partial harm first; under a loss that charges it, every positive verdict here
weakens — the negative ones are robust).

**Dropped, with reasons (referee findings).** The single-mixture microfoundation
generating all three observables from one weight set [COMP] — quantitatively
self-contradictory (σ ≈ 4.7 vs ≈1 across channels). The smooth Hill onset h ≈ 1.5
as the exit shape [FT, RATES] — rejected 2ΔNLL = 18.3 against the step. The "no win
at any dose" corollary [FT] — proved only at B = 1000; mid-dose emptiness does not
follow and is exactly what §6.3 tests. The per-token prefix lever κ* ≈ 0.10 [FT] —
contradicted by the stack entry accounting (4.5–5.2σ); retained only as the
alternative branch of §6.2. Rate language (γ, ε) for the LLM — the non-ergodic
discriminators say the learning-time objects are mass shares, not switching rates;
the proved process-level threshold (R_M = β/γ, prior sprint C1/C2) remains the
generation-time ancestor, recovered only as the ergodic special case.
