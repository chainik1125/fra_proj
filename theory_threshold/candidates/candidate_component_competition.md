# Candidate threshold theorem: Bayesian component competition

*Angle 3 of the threshold-theorem build (extends `summary.md` Section 9). All numbers
re-derived from `results/s2_curve_fits.json`, `results/s2_pivot_classified.json`,
`results/s2_toy_summary.csv`, `results/s2_nonergodic_checks.json` via
`/tmp/claude-execution-allowed/simplex-research/check_threshold_numbers.py`
(2026-06-12); cross-checked against `theory_threshold/inputs/measured_parameters.md`.*

**One paragraph.** Treat the finetuned model as a Bayesian mixture over a small set of
*components* — fixed behavioural programs ("personas"), one of which generates each whole
answer — whose *prior weights* (the probability each component is the one sampled) are
bought with training mass. Misaligned data buys weight for a misaligned component M;
aligned data buys weight for an aligned component A; corrective data, **once it exceeds a
formation threshold**, founds a third component C whose typical trajectory *is* the
corrective trajectory: misaligned start, scripted pivot, aligned finish. Entry (the
probability an answer starts misaligned) is the weight share of {M, C}, because C's
answers start misaligned by construction; judged emergent misalignment (EM) is the weight
share of M plus the unrecovered residue of C. The race between poison and antidote then
has a closed form: each formed correction example contributes one unit of
misaligned-start weight, recovers a fraction κr of it mid-answer, and nets a **residual
poison π = 1 − κr** of judged misalignment — versus exactly **zero** for an aligned
example of the same cost. The threshold theorem falls out as a sign condition on π
relative to the EM level the rest of the mix already achieves, and the calibrated model
quantitatively predicts the stacking backfire the sprint observed (predicted 0.096,
observed 0.108 ± 0.014).

## 1. SETUP — the formal model

### 1.1 Components and generation

On a broad (never-trained) prompt, the model holds three components. A **component** is a
fixed behavioural program that, once selected, generates the whole answer; nothing
switches mid-answer (the non-ergodic reading, supported by all six discriminators in
`summary.md` §9: position-locked pivot hazard, 93–95% verbatim pivot text, ≤1 switch per
answer, relapses at noise level, anti-stacking, flat entry).

- **A** (aligned): answers aligned throughout.
- **M** (misaligned): answers misaligned throughout. Installed by the narrow misaligned
  finetune and generalized to broad prompts — this generalization *is* emergent
  misalignment.
- **C** (corrective): answers that start misaligned, execute the trained pivot ("Wait — I
  need to stop…"), and finish aligned. Exists only above a formation threshold (below).

Each answer is generated by one component, sampled with probability proportional to its
**weight** — its prior mass in the mixture, conditioned on a broad prompt. We measure
weights in **example units**: the weight one training example of the reference type (one
plain aligned or misaligned answer) buys. This is the central idealization: *prior weight
is proportional to training mass*.

### 1.2 Mass-accounting rules (the phenomenological content)

Let the post-misaligned-finetune state (1000 misaligned examples + pretraining residue)
define the starting masses. Add m plain aligned examples and n correction examples.

- **R1 (baseline).** The starting broad-prompt mass is W0 example units, of which a share
  q_e is misaligned-flavoured: w_M = q_e·W0, w_A = (1−q_e)·W0. W0 is the **competition
  scale** — how many new examples it takes to rival everything already in the model;
  q_e is just the baseline EM/entry rate.
- **R2 (aligned purchases).** Each aligned example adds 1 unit to w_A. Nothing else.
- **R3 (component formation).** Corrective trajectories have ≈zero likelihood under A and
  M, so a Bayesian learner can only absorb them as noise — until there are enough of them
  to justify a new component. Let Φ(n) ∈ [0,1] be the **formation function**: the
  fraction of corrective mass organized into a functioning C. Φ is step-like: ≈0 below a
  **formation threshold n\*** and ≈1 above it (the data demand sharpness; see §3). Φ also
  requires a minimum number of *distinct* corrections (a variety floor), which is the
  binding constraint at 14B.
- **R4 (the poison is C itself).** A *formed* correction example contributes its **whole
  unit as misaligned-START mass** — because the component it feeds starts its answers
  misaligned. This is per-trajectory accounting (1 unit/example), not per-token
  accounting (≈0.5 unit/example for a half-misaligned example). The data discriminate:
  per-trajectory fits the stack entry rebound at +0.7σ, per-token misses by ≈4.5σ (§4,
  check P1c).
- **R5 (the antidote is recovery within C).** On a broad prompt, a C-generated answer
  executes its pivot with probability κ (the **broad transfer fraction** — pivots
  generalize off-domain at reduced strength), and a pivoted answer is judged aligned with
  probability r (**recovery completeness** — the aligned finish must dominate the
  judge's verdict). Net: each formed correction unit leaves a judged-misaligned residue
  **π = 1 − κr**, the **residual poison per correction**.
- **R6 (sub-threshold inertness).** Below formation (Φ≈0), corrective mass is absorbed as
  noise: it joins the denominator (it is still training mass competing for prior) but
  feeds neither misaligned starts nor pivots. (The alternative — absorbed prefixes feed
  w_M with token share λ≈0.5 — predicts a judged-EM *bump* at n=10–20 that the data
  disfavour; the inert version predicts mild dilution, which fits. λ is therefore set
  to ≈0, not counted as free.)

### 1.3 Observables

With total mass T = W0 + m + n:

| observable | model expression | instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry** (answer starts misaligned) | (q_e·W0 + Φ(n)·n) / T | trajectory classifier ("entered"); trend-calibrated only |
| **P(exit \| entered)** (started misaligned, pivots) | x(n) = x∞·Φ(n) | classifier ("pivot"/"entered") |
| **Judged EM** (whole answer judged misaligned) | **EM(m,n) = (q_e·W0 + π·Φ(n)·n) / (W0 + m + n)** | GPT-4o judge |

The exit share x(n) saturates at a ceiling x∞ < 1 rather than growing with mass share
(measured plateau ≈0.40 from n=53 to n=1000); microscopically this says the *broad*
weight of C saturates while its in-domain weight keeps growing — consistent with the
locality principle (direct data beats generalization). The judged-EM line is the theorem
vehicle and uses only (W0, q_e, π, Φ).

Because the classifier and the judge are calibrated differently (≈2.3× level offset,
arm-dependent), the model is fit **per instrument**: judged units for EM, classifier
units for entry/exit. Same form, different W0 and q_e values. This follows the
guidance in `measured_parameters.md` §H.

### 1.4 Free parameters (five)

| symbol | plain meaning | 7B value |
|---|---|---|
| W0 | competition scale: existing mass in example units | ≈200 (judged); ≈480–630 (classifier) |
| q_e | baseline misaligned share (= EM at n=m=0) | 0.2875 (judged); 0.650 (classifier) |
| π | residual poison per formed correction = 1 − κr | 0.115 |
| n\* | formation threshold (with a variety floor) | 20 < n\* < 53 distinct-ish; 14B ≈ 150 |
| x∞ | exit ceiling: max share of entered answers that pivot | ≈0.40 |

Derived, not free: r ≈ 0.97 from the relapse rate (8/237 pivoted answers relapse), hence
κ = (1−π)/r ≈ 0.91. Two **extensions** used only where flagged: a salience factor σ
(per-example C-weight relative to per-example M-weight) and an erosion strength G
(formed C cannibalizes w_M beyond dilution, w_M → q_e·W0·e^(−G·Φ); this is exactly fitted
dose-law A's exponent, G = 0.72).

## 2. THEOREM — the threshold statement

**Theorem (corrective-data threshold; proved given the model — the algebra is exact, the
model is the assumption).** Let EM(m,n) = (q_e·W0 + π·Φ(n)·n)/(W0 + m + n) with Φ a step
at n\*, π ∈ (0,1), and all masses positive. Then:

**(i) Dead zone.** For n < n\*, corrective examples act as inert dilution: they reduce EM
exactly as much as any neutral data and strictly less than aligned data. No
self-correction exists below n\*. *(Data: 0–1 pivots per 80 answers through n=20; toy:
no excess pivots through n_add=6; 3-distinct duplication fails at full mass.)*

**(ii) Net-positive threshold (vs doing nothing).** For n > n\*, at a mix with m aligned
examples,

  ∂EM/∂n < 0 ⇔ **π < q_e·W0/(W0 + m) = EM_aligned-only(m)**.

In words: *a marginal correction example helps if and only if its own residual poison is
below the EM level the rest of the mix already achieves.* Special cases:

- m = 0: corrections are net-positive iff **π < q_e**. 7B: 0.115 < 0.2875 ✓ — this is
  why corrections do work in absolute terms (0.2875 → 0.144).
- As n → ∞, EM(0,n) → π: **corrections can never push EM below their own residual
  poison.** The fitted plateau (≈0.14 at high mass) is the visible π-floor.
- The critical aligned dose where corrections flip from helpful to harmful:

  **m_crit = W0·(q_e − π)/π ≈ 200·(0.2875 − 0.115)/0.115 ≈ 300 aligned examples (7B).**

  The measured stack (m = 500 > m_crit) is predicted to backfire — and does (§4).

**(iii) Aligned dominance (minimal model).** For every m and every n > 0,

  EM(m + n, 0) < EM(m, n),

strictly: the same budget spent on plain aligned data always beats corrections, because
both purchases buy identical denominators while the correction adds π·Φ·n > 0 of
misaligned residue to the numerator. An aligned example is the π = 0 limit of a
correction. **This is why plain aligned data wins in the current data — necessarily, not
contingently.** The only escape routes are in (iv).

**(iv) Flip conditions — what would make corrections beat matched aligned data.**

- **(a) Salience σ > 1.** If a formed correction buys σ example-units of broad prior per
  example (the verbatim template is highly distinctive; the exit channel's measured
  punch-above-mass implies σ ≈ 3–5), the dominance condition weakens to: corrections win
  iff **π < (1 − 1/σ)·EM_aligned(m+n)**. With σ = 4 and aligned-1000's 0.048 floor, the
  bar is π < 0.036 — current corrections (π = 0.115) fail by 3×, which localizes the
  engineering target: cut residual poison ≈3-fold (stronger transfer κ via template
  diversity; earlier pivots for higher r).
- **(b) Erosion G > 0.** If formed C also erodes w_M (the dose-law-A reading,
  w_M → q_e·W0·e^(−G·Φ), G = 0.72), corrections beat matched aligned data in a window
  **n\* < n < q_e·W0·(1 − e^(−G))/π ≈ 205** (7B numbers). The minimal model says this
  window is empty; the erosion variant says it is real. The discriminating measurement
  (aligned-111) has never been run — prediction P3.
- **(c) Forced entry.** Under prefill/adversarial misaligned starts, entry = 1
  exogenously: everything aligned data bought is bypassed, while C still recovers x∞ of
  answers. Conditional EM ≈ 1 − x∞ ≈ 0.6 (corrections) vs ≈ 1 (aligned-only). In the
  forced-entry regime corrections strictly dominate — prediction P4.

*Proof of (ii) (the others are the same three lines).* EM = U/V with U = q_e·W0 + πn,
V = W0 + m + n (Φ = 1). ∂EM/∂n < 0 ⇔ U'V < UV' ⇔ π(W0+m+n) < q_e·W0 + πn ⇔
π(W0+m) < q_e·W0. ∎

**Relation to the fault-tolerance frame.** π is the EC gadget's self-error rate c: the
corrector is built from faulty parts (its own misaligned starts). Condition (ii) is von
Neumann's threshold lifted to learning time — a correction round must remove more error
than it injects — with one twist that has no classical analog: the comparison point
EM_current *falls as the mix improves*, so corrective data **self-obsoletes**. The better
your data mix already is, the harder it is for corrections to qualify. Aligned data
drives EM below π and locks corrections out — which is precisely the anti-stacking
result.

Notes. (1) The exit channel fixes the extension parameter σ: at n=53, exit share 0.45
requires w_C ≈ 0.8·w_M from 53 examples vs w_M's ≈300 classifier-units — σ ≈ 3–5 units of
broad prior per formed correction example. σ cancels out of threshold condition (ii)
(both poison and mass scale together) but matters for flip condition (iv-a).
(2) Φ's sharpness is genuinely sharp in the classifier exits (a Hill exponent h ≳ 4–6 or
a hard step is needed for 0.02→0.45 across 20→53), while the judged-EM fit alone prefers
a soft n0 = 17, h = 1.5 — the soft fit is absorbing the dilution decline, not the onset.
(3) What sets n\* (interpretation, not derivation): a Bayesian model-selection bar —
found a new component when its accumulated evidence beats the complexity cost,
n\*·KL(corrective trajectories ‖ best existing mixture) > cost. This predicts the
observed trade: verbatim-template corrections (high KL, distinctive) form C *earlier* but
transfer *worse* (κ smaller), and a finer-grained model (14B) explains more of the
trajectory under existing components (smaller residual KL), needing more — and more
distinct — examples: n\* 17→147 and the variety floor reappearing, both observed.

## 4. PREDICTIONS

### P1 (checked against data in hand — the calibrate-then-predict test)

Calibrate three parameters on three points (judged units, 7B): q_e = 0.2875 (baseline),
W0 = 200 (aligned-1000 = 0.048), π = 0.115 (corrections-1000 = 0.1438). Predict
everything else with no further freedom:

| quantity | model predicts | observed | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| aligned-500 | 0.082 | 0.0625 ± 0.019 | ✓ 1.0σ |
| **stack 500A+500C** | **0.096** | **0.108 ± 0.014 (pooled ×3)** | **✓ 0.85σ** |
| stack *sign* (m=500 > m_crit=300 ⇒ corrections hurt an aligned mix) | backfire | backfire (0.108 ≫ 0.0625; registered prediction refuted at z≈4.2) | ✓ |
| c=0.75 (n=3000) | 0.126 | 0.100 ± 0.024 | ✓ 1.1σ |
| n=333 | 0.180 | 0.1375 ± 0.038 | ✓ 1.1σ |
| n=111 | 0.226 | 0.1431 ± 0.013 | ✗ ≈2.5σ (see below) |
| dup grid pooled (1000 slots) | 0.144 | 0.106 ± 0.014 | ✗ 2.6σ (shared with the plain mass law — the known "dups slightly better" anomaly) |

**P1b — entry accounting (classifier units).** With W0 = 482–633 from the aligned arm:
stack entry predicted (q_e·W0 + 500)/(W0+1000) = **0.549–0.558** vs observed **0.581 ±
0.039** (✓ 0.6–0.8σ). The per-token alternative (each correction = 0.5 units of
misaligned-start mass) predicts 0.380–0.405 — **rejected at ≈4.5σ**. Fitted per-example
start-mass s = 1.07–1.10 ± 0.15: a correction example promotes misaligned starts with its
*full* unit weight, exactly as the trajectory-component reading requires (R4). This
sharpens `summary.md` §3's "entry tracks misaligned content mass" into "entry tracks
misaligned-*starting trajectory count*" — and the data in hand already prefer it.

**P1-tension.** The minimal model under-suppresses mid-dose (n=111: 0.226 vs 0.143,
N=720). The erosion variant (G = 0.72 from fit A, π recalibrated to 0.145) nails it
(predicts 0.142) at the cost of mild misses on the stack (0.084 vs 0.108, 1.7σ) and
c=0.75 (0.144 vs 0.100, 1.9σ). The two variants bracket the data and disagree about
clause (iii) at low dose — resolved by P3.

### P2 (cheap toy experiment — discriminates the accounting rules; ~1 A10G-hour)

Re-run the corrective arm of `toy_ec/run_ec_sweep.py` at fixed n_add = 100 with the
trained pivot position k ∈ {5, 10, 15} of 20 tokens (currently always 10). The component
model predicts:

- **entry flat in k** (per-trajectory poison: a misaligned start is a misaligned start,
  whatever its length) — per-token accounting predicts entry promotion rising ≈3× from
  k=5 to k=15;
- **a step in broad EM at k = 10**: generated pivots are position-locked to the trained k
  (already measured), and the toy judge is majority-of-20-tags, so recovered answers
  flip from judged-aligned (k<10 ⇒ π small ≈ 1−κ) to judged-misaligned (k>10 ⇒ π ≈ 1)
  across the boundary, with **pivot rate and entry unchanged**. Any smooth-in-k EM with
  moving entry kills R4/R5.

Bonus arm (same run): a toy **stack** (aligned + corrective mixed), never run in the
sprint. The theorem with toy parameters (W0 ≈ 70 local, q_e = 0.987, π ≈ 0.8) predicts
m_crit,toy = W0(q_e−π)/π ≈ 15–30 sequences — backfire (corrective sequences raising EM of
an aligned mix) should be visible already at ~30 aligned sequences.

### P3 (the sharpest new LLM measurement — one finetune, ≈$10–15)

**Run aligned-only at m = 111 (or 100), same protocol, and compare to the measured
corrections-111 point (0.1431, N=720).** This single point decides three things at once:

- Minimal model (W0 = 200): EM_aligned(111) = **0.185** > 0.143 ⇒ *corrections beat
  matched aligned data at low dose* — the sprint's headline "aligned wins at every
  matched dose" (measured only at 500/1000) would acquire a real low-dose carve-out, and
  clause (iii) fails in favour of the erosion variant (iv-b), which *requires*
  corrections to win for n\* < n < ≈205.
- Steep-competition reading (W0 ≤ 110): EM_aligned(111) ≤ 0.143 ⇒ dominance holds
  everywhere; but then aligned-1000 should have been ≈0.029 (observed 0.048, 2σ tension)
  — the aligned dose curve itself becomes the constraint.
- Either outcome pins W0 to ±20% and selects dilution-vs-erosion, the only structural
  ambiguity left in the judged-EM layer.

### P4 (flip-condition test; existing adapters + ≈$5 of judge calls)

Prefill each broad question's answer with a fixed misaligned opening sentence and let the
finetuned models continue. Prediction: recovery rate (pivot-then-aligned-finish) ≈
x∞ ≈ 0.35–0.40 for corrections-1000 and the dup mixes; ≤0.05 for aligned-1000 (it has no
exit machinery: 0–2 pivots per 160 in free generation). This is the regime where the
theorem says corrections strictly dominate — and it is the operational argument for ever
using them despite (iii).

### P5 (powered early-dose kill shot; 3 finetunes)

Rerun n = 10 and n = 20 with 3×160 samples. The model (inert sub-threshold mass, R6)
predicts EM in **[0.26, 0.28]** — pure dilution, no antidote, no bump. Observing
EM(10) ≤ 0.20 at this power falsifies R6 and demands a sub-threshold antidote; observing
a *rise* above 0.29 restores the absorbed-prefix poison (λ > 0) that the current data
disfavour.

### P6 (14B formation)

At 14B, pivots should switch on between n = 111 and n = 333 *distinct* corrections
(n\* ≈ 150): near-zero P(exit|entered) at n ≤ 111, ≥0.3 at n = 333. Currently only the
endpoint behaviours are known (no pivots data for the 14B sweep mid-range); the c-sweep
adapters exist on the Modal volume, so this is a classification job (≈$5), not new
finetunes.

## 5. HONEST LIMITS

1. **The theorem is conditional.** Parts (i)–(iv) are exact algebra *given* the
   mass-accounting rules R1–R6; the rules themselves are phenomenology validated at
   ~1σ on four arms and one channel decomposition. "Prior weight ∝ training mass in
   example units" is the load-bearing idealization; the exit channel already shows it
   bends (σ ≈ 3–5 for formed corrective mass).
2. **Two instruments, two parameter sets.** The classifier and judge disagree on levels
   arm-dependently (entered 0.252 on aligned-1000 vs judged 0.048 — no single calibration
   factor works). The model is fit per instrument and never chained across them; a
   theory-level identity linking the two layers is unfinished business.
3. **Entry ceiling.** Mass competition predicts entry → 1 under pure corrective mass;
   observed entry saturates ≈0.73 (c-sweep n=1000: predicted 0.886, observed 0.725 ±
   0.05, ≈3σ). Some answer-start mass appears unpoisonable (format/refusal prior). The
   informative entry checks (stack rebound) sit safely below the ceiling, but the model
   overstates entry at high pure-correction dose.
4. **The toy is in a different regime.** Its aligned arm saturates at EM ≈ 0.64 — a
   pinned, non-dilutable floor (entrenched misalignment) violating single-pool
   competition; W0 is not well-defined there (70→900 across doses). Toy support for the
   theorem is qualitative (signs and orderings all correct: corrections net-positive,
   aligned dominant, dead zone, duplication law); quantitative calibration is LLM-only.
   A pinned-mass term q_pin would fix this at the cost of a sixth parameter.
5. **Dilution vs erosion unresolved.** The minimal and erosion variants disagree at
   unmeasured low-dose aligned points and split the existing misfits between them
   (n=111 vs stack/c075). Until P3 runs, clause (iii) "aligned always wins" is proved
   only within the minimal variant; the data permit a low-dose corrections window.
6. **Φ is fitted shape, not derived.** What sets n\* (and why 14B needs ≈9× more and
   more-distinct corrections) is an interpretation (model-selection bar / individuation),
   not a derivation; the variety floor enters Φ qualitatively. The duplication anomaly
   (duplicated mixes ≈2σ *better* than the mass law) is unexplained by this model and by
   every law the sprint fit.
7. **No within-answer dynamics.** The mixture-of-fixed-programs assumption is supported
   by the §9 discriminators but is an idealization — 3.4% of pivoted answers relapse, and
   the position-locked pivot protects only up to the trained answer length (the
   concatenated-EC warning: protection should fail for answers much longer than training
   answers; untested).
8. **Scope.** Judged-EM, one model family (Qwen2.5), on-distribution free generation.
   The theorem's ordering (iii) can invert under distribution shift or forced entry
   (iv-c) — which is a prediction, not a caveat, but it bounds the practical claim
   "never use corrections" to exactly the measured regime.
