**Strongest:** The hard-cut is a legitimate, faithful cell-removal, not a degenerate -inf-on-edge edit. hard_cut_hooks (lines 224-238) sets hook_attn_scores[answer_row, kE]=-1e4 and [answer_row, kV]=-1e4 — a standard 2-cell attention-knockout at exactly the answer-query row (textbook path-patching). It lands at supp=0.848, sib_coll=0.0021, degen=0.083, i.e. the model still emits well-formed names in ~92% of cases. It faithfully implements "the answer position cannot attend to the target entity/value tokens." The finite-c messiness does NOT impugn this: the two FRA interventions are genuinely DIFFERENT operations, not one scaled. build_fra_delta (lines 288-289) accumulates the selected feature-pairs' contributions at ALL query rows on the kE/kV columns (no qq==qpos constraint), so large c drives the whole kE/kV columns negative across the prompt -> global softmax disruption (sib_coll -> 0.95 at c=8), while it only subtracts top-M=24 approximate pairs so target-supp plateaus at <=0.105. The non-monotonic curve (sib 0.95 at c=8 then 0.002 at c=1e9) is two distinct edits on one axis, not a single intervention's pathology. The evaluator's "softmax-overdrive red-herring" call is correct in dropping the scaled curve, but the honest reason is "different, row-spread, partial-reconstruction edit," not just overdrive.

**Next test:** Add a faithful FINITE cell-removal arm to the harness and re-run: in build_fra_delta restrict the delta accumulation to qq==qpos only (change line 288 to also require d["qq"][n]==qpos), then sweep c in {1,2,4,8,...} until target-supp matches t*=0.80, reading sib_coll. If this answer-row-only, faithfully-targeted finite edit reaches high supp with low collateral (curve approaching the hard-cut's 0.002, not the all-rows 0.95), it confirms the hard-cut == clean cell-removal and the win is intrinsic. If it ALSO plateaus at supp~0.10, that proves the top-M=24 reconstruction is too partial and the win genuinely requires the binary clamp — still legitimate but worth stating as "the win lives at the c->inf knockout, finite FRA reconstruction cannot reach it."

**Bottom line:** The 11x is robust on the legitimacy axis I own: the hard-cut is a clean, well-defined 2-cell attention-knockout at the answer row (supp 0.848, sib 0.002, degen 0.083), not a degenerate -inf edit, and the linear gets full layer/alpha tuning and still loses by 11x. The flagged oddity is fully explained: the messy finite-c curve is a DIFFERENT intervention (subtracts a partial reconstruction across all query rows, not just the answer row), so its non-monotonicity is apples-to-oranges, not a pathology of cell-removal — the win does not depend on it. The one honest caveat (minor, and a fairness rather than legitimacy issue): the FRA "curve" is effectively a single binary operating point — the matched-point interpolation at t*=0.80 puts ~94% weight on the oracle — so the 11x is operating-point-specific to high suppression where the linear's broad direction is forced into degeneracy; the fair magnitude is the interpolated 11x (the 340x "oracle-point" figure is a discrete-knockout-vs-continuous-magnitude comparison and should not be headlined).

**Bottom line:** The FRA hard-cut IS a real, clean edit (supp 0.848 at coll 0.0021, degen 0.083) that the linear-as-built genuinely cannot match without degenerating — so this is NOT a pure artifact and FRA does have a selectivity edge.
