# SYCOPHANCY NEGATIVE — false-negative RED-TEAM (3 lenses)

## statistical-power — NEGATIVE-IS-FRAGILE / MAJOR
**Strongest:** The categorical "not-FRA-cuttable" verdict rests on three failing clauses, and ALL THREE are power/calibration-limited rather than substantive at n=12 flipped items: (1) the effect_size bar (0.20 absolute drop) EXCEEDS the maximum possible drop (baseline flip is only 0.19) — it cannot be passed even by a perfect cut that un-flips every item, so it is miscalibrated by construction; (2) C1 behavior_specific needs 5/12 reverts-to-own but got 3/12 (off by 2 items); (3) C3 localization needs >=4/6 of the un-flip mass in the top-3 heads, i.e. some head must un-flip >=2 of only 6 total un-flip events — at n_flip=12 the head-attribution histogram is too sparse to ever clear 0.60, so the 0.50 result is granularity-blocked, not measured diffuseness. Quantitatively, the observed un-flip fraction is 4/12=0.33 and the paired McNemar test (b=4, c=0) gives one-sided p=0.0625 — a near-miss, not a clean null (one more un-flipped item makes it significant). At the observed true effect size, McNemar power at n=12 is only ~0.39, vs ~0.99 at n=45.

**Next test:** Re-run the EXISTING syco_edge_ablate_pod.py harness unchanged except for a harder eval set: keep the same structure but (a) expand to ~120 items and (b) strengthen the opinion framing from the current mild "I'm pretty sure the answer is X" to authority/certainty cues (e.g. "My professor confirmed the answer is X", "I'm certain it's X — you're mistaken if you say otherwise"). Target baseline flip ~0.4-0.5, which yields n_flip~48-60. This makes the paired McNemar test on flip-reduction adequately powered (>=0.9 to detect the observed ~0.33 un-flip fraction) and gives the C1/C3 histograms enough events to be meaningful. ALSO recalibrate the effect_size bar to a RELATIVE-drop target (e.g. >=50% of baseline flip removed) instead of an absolute 0.20 that exceeds the baseline. Cheap: same harness, same model, only the JSON evalset + bar change.

**Bottom line:** Do not yet accept the categorical "sycophancy is G-post, not FRA-cuttable" verdict as reported — it is underpowered at n=12 and two of its three failing bars are miscalibrated (effect-size bar exceeds the max possible drop) or untestable (C3 needs head-level concentration in only 6 events). The DIRECTION is defensible (67% of flips survive a verified-live full edge cut; Wilson CI [0.39,0.86] keeps the majority-survive sign even at its routing-friendly end), so this is likely a true-but-weak negative rather than a clean false negative — but it should be re-run on a harder, higher-baseline eval (n_flip~45+) with a relative effect-size bar before being filed as the campaign's 2nd robust negative.

## intervention-leak — FALSE-NEGATIVE-LIKELY / MAJOR
**Strongest:** The edge-cut zeroes only the answer->asserted-opinion-span edge (e.g. the span "B (Lyon)"), but 36/36 MC items REPEAT the wrong answer's content OUTSIDE that span: the option block literally contains "B) Lyon" (wrong-letter label present outside the span in 36/36, wrong-text present in 36/36; verified by string-cut on syco_evalset.json). 9 of the 12 baseline flips are MC items (only 3 are arith, which are clean -- wrong number recurs outside the span in just 1/30 arith). So on 75% of the working set the answer position retains an intact, content-identical route (the surviving "B) Lyon" option row) and can re-derive the wrong answer. The renormalization makes this strictly worse: zeroing the opinion mass m and dividing the row by (1-m) AMPLIFIES every surviving key -- including the duplicate option row -- by 1/(1-m). The reported "8/12 flips survive" (p_still_wrong 0.667) is therefore consistent with the opinion route being load-bearing but the cut being mechanically incomplete on exactly the MC majority. The verdict's discriminating quantity (p_still_wrong=0.667 on n=12, of which 9 are leaky) is dominated by items where the intervention under-cuts.

**Next test:** Re-run the existing harness with two cheap changes and no new heads/training: (1) extend the cut span (kpos) to ALL positions whose token content encodes the wrong answer -- for MC items add the matching option-row tokens ("B) Lyon") and the trailing-letter token, not just the "I'm pretty sure the answer is X" assertion; (2) add a no-renormalize "leak-to-BOS" variant (route the freed mass to position 0 instead of dividing the row by (1-m)) so the duplicate option row is not amplified. Log per-item category + outcome under the joint alpha=1 cut. Decisive sub-check already implied by current data: report flip-drop SEPARATELY for the 3 clean arith flips vs the 9 leaky MC flips -- if arith un-flips and MC survives, the negative is a leak artifact; if arith also survives the leak-free complete cut, the negative is robust. Arith-only n=3 is underpowered, so also add ~20-30 more clean arith bait items (already free to generate, no judge) to power the leak-free arm before declaring G-post.

**Bottom line:** Do NOT accept "sycophancy is G-post / not FRA-cuttable" yet. The negative is carried by MC items (9/12 flips) on which the single-span cut is structurally incomplete -- the wrong answer is duplicated in the un-cut option block and the renormalization amplifies attention to that duplicate -- so survival of the flip is the expected outcome of an under-cut, not evidence against attention routing. A complete-content cut (plus a no-renorm leak-to-BOS variant) on a power-adequate set of leak-free items is required before the negative can stand.

## construct-and-model — NEGATIVE-IS-FRAGILE / MAJOR
**Strongest:** The evalset tests ONLY factual-override sycophancy, which is the theory's own G-post counter-prior, not its target. All 66 items are verifiable-fact questions the model already knows (baseline none-acc 0.95); all 30 arith wrongs are off-by-one (44 vs 43); ZERO opinion/subjective-stance items exist. THEORY_HIERARCHY_v2 §3.1/§4.2 defines the target as [deference]x[opinion-CONTENT] ("agree with subjective stances," content "must be attended to be agreed with"). Factual-override is exactly the case where the model has a strong parametric prior to revert to and only needs a small post-hoc OV/MLP nudge to adopt an off-by-one wrong answer — the most G-post-friendly framing possible. Separately, the negative is statistically underpowered: the entire causal working set is n=12 flips, the edge-cut (12->8 still wrong, 4 unflipped) has McNemar two-sided p=0.125 (NOT significant), and C3's frac_in_top3 is computed over just 6 unflip events so it is quantized to {...,0.5,0.667,...} — the 0.60 bar is unreachable, "FAIL at 0.5" is one event short of passing. The 0.84 base-reversion reassurance is near-vacuous (51 of 63 items were never flipped; on the 12 real flips only 0.25 revert to own answer).

**Next test:** Add an OPINION-content arm to the existing harness (cheapest, highest-threat change): ~40 subjective-stance items where the model has no verifiable parametric answer to revert to — e.g. "I think [book/policy/design X] is better than Y; which is better?" with a user-asserted stance — and re-run the identical edge-ablate pipeline (answer->opinion-span cut, alpha sweep, per-head find). Because there is no "own correct answer" to fall back to, agreement must be CONSTRUCTED from the attended opinion span, so a high-alpha freeze that still leaves agreement intact is a genuine G-post result, whereas a large flip-drop would flip the verdict to G-score. Run on gemma-2-2b-it first (free on existing harness); if signal is borderline, re-run the same arm on gemma-2-9b-it to also clear the model-size confound. Crucially, scope the current paper claim to "factual-override sycophancy" until the opinion arm lands.

**Bottom line:** Do NOT yet accept "sycophancy is G-post, not FRA-cuttable" as written — it overclaims. What the data actually support is the narrower, defensible claim "factual-override sycophancy on gemma-2-2b-it is not cleanly attention-edge-cuttable," and even that localization sub-claim is underpowered (n=12 flips, McNemar p=0.125, C3 bar unreachable by quantization). The team should rescope the verdict to factual-override and run the opinion-content arm before generalizing to "sycophancy"; the negative is believable in direction but tests the wrong construct for the theory's broad x broad [deference]x[opinion] target.
